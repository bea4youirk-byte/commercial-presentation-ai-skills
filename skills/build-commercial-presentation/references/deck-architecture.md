# Commercial deck architecture

Choose the shortest useful sequence; do not force every block into every deck.

1. Decision context: who the proposal is for and what decision is being made.
2. Stakes: the operational or commercial consequence of the current state.
3. Requirements: agreed loads, constraints, priorities, audience needs, or success criteria.
4. Recommended approach: the core solution and why it fits.
5. Configuration or scope: what is included and how the parts work together.
6. Outcome: what changes for the buyer, with assumptions and limits.
7. Evidence: specifications, calculations, cases, process, certifications, or team competence.
8. Options: only when the buyer genuinely needs a comparison.
9. Commercial terms: price, scope, exclusions, delivery dependencies, validity, and payment logic.
10. Risk control: warranty, responsibilities, acceptance, service, and unresolved verification items.
11. Next step: one specific action, owner, and required inputs.

For technical offers, keep system limits visible. An orientation size is not a final engineering selection. For a manufacturer deck, distinguish manufacturer claims from the seller's own obligations.
