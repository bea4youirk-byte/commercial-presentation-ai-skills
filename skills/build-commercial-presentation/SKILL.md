# Build a commercial presentation

Build a decision instrument, not a company brochure.

## Define the sale

Identify the buyer, buying situation, decision makers, problem, cost of inaction, desired outcome, objections, decision criteria, offer, scope, evidence, and next action. Separate facts supplied by the user from assumptions and research.

Write the communication job in one sentence: by the end, the buyer should choose or approve a specific next step because the proposed solution fits their situation and the evidence supports it.

Read [references/deck-architecture.md](references/deck-architecture.md) before outlining. Use [references/claim-discipline.md](references/claim-discipline.md) whenever the deck includes specifications, prices, savings, warranties, schedules, comparisons, or regulated claims.

## Build the argument

Use only the slides needed to resolve the buying decision. Give each slide one claim and an action-oriented title. Connect customer context to consequences, solution, configuration, scope, proof, commercial terms, risk control, and next step.

Do not lead with company history, generic mission language, an equipment catalog, or unsupported superlatives. Translate features into buyer outcomes while retaining the technical facts needed for trust.

Keep alternatives comparable. State inclusions, exclusions, dependencies, assumptions, validity periods, and what requires a survey or final calculation.

## Create the deck

Use the platform's native presentation capability. When a source template exists, inherit and edit its layouts rather than recreating its style. When no template exists, establish a restrained visual system appropriate to the audience.

Use authentic product images and evidence. Do not generate fake projects, screenshots, testimonials, logos, certificates, or installation photos.

## Verify before delivery

Check every claim against its source, render every slide, inspect fit and hierarchy at full size, and test the editable output. The closing slide must state one concrete next step with the information or approval required from the buyer.
