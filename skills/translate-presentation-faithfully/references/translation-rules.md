# Translation rules

## Protected content

Keep unchanged unless the source explicitly localizes it:

- company and product trademarks;
- manufacturer and model names;
- SKUs, part numbers, firmware versions, protocol names, URLs, emails, and QR codes;
- numeric values, mathematical operators, chart data, formulas, test conditions, and certification identifiers;
- standard symbols and SI units.

Do not convert currencies, units, decimal separators, date formats, or regulatory terminology unless the user requests localization beyond translation. If conversion is requested, retain the source value and state the conversion basis.

## Technical content

Preserve qualifiers such as nominal, maximum, continuous, peak, usable, recommended, optional, typical, up to, under test conditions, and subject to configuration. Never turn a range into a single value or an option into an included feature.

Keep electrical distinctions exact: power versus energy, AC versus DC, single-phase versus three-phase, input versus output, rated versus maximum, capacity versus current, efficiency versus yield.

Use accepted target-language terminology. When a term has no stable equivalent, retain the original in parentheses on first use.

## Tables and charts

Translate headers, legends, axis titles, annotations, footnotes, and explanatory labels. Do not change values, series order, scales, formulas, sorting, colors, or chart type.

Treat embedded spreadsheet data as protected. Verify that exported charts still point to the same data.

## Images containing text

Do not replace or regenerate product photography. If text is baked into an image, prefer an editable source asset. Otherwise preserve the image and add a faithful localized overlay only when the user authorizes it and the overlay can be placed without covering product details. Record the exception.

## Claims and legal text

Translate claims, warranties, safety warnings, certifications, and disclaimers conservatively. Preserve scope, conditions, exclusions, and jurisdiction references. Do not strengthen or soften claims.

## Source problems

When the source contains a likely typo, conflicting specification, missing unit, or mistranslation, do not fix it silently. Keep the source meaning and add it to the exception log with slide number and exact location.
