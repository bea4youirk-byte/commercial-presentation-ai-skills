# Audit a presentation

Diagnose and report. Do not edit unless the user asks for fixes.

## Inspect the full artifact

Determine the audience, decision, intended outcome, source constraints, and whether the deck is new, translated, or template-based. Inspect every slide at full size, the editable object structure, masters/layouts, notes, tables, charts, links, media, fonts, and export behavior.

Read [references/audit-scorecard.md](references/audit-scorecard.md). Apply only relevant categories; do not punish a technical reference deck for lacking an emotional sales arc.

## Validate content and evidence

Trace non-trivial claims, numbers, specifications, comparisons, savings, prices, schedules, warranties, certifications, and cases to sources. Recalculate totals and chart relationships where possible. Mark unsupported claims and distinguish blockers from optional improvements.

For translated decks, compare source and target slide by slide. For template-based decks, check inherited structure, protected objects, brand marks, fonts, placeholders, and visual deviations.

## Report priorities

Lead with the overall verdict and the few issues most likely to damage comprehension, trust, conversion, or file usability. Classify findings:

- blocker: cannot safely send or present;
- high: materially weakens the decision or credibility;
- medium: noticeable but recoverable;
- low: polish.

For each finding give slide, evidence, consequence, and exact recommended change. Separate verified defects from subjective preferences. End with a send/no-send recommendation and the smallest repair sequence.
