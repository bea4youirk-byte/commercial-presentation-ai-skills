# Build a marketing presentation

Start with audience behavior and evidence, not decorative slides.

## Define the communication job

Identify the audience, awareness stage, current belief or behavior, desired belief or action, tension, proof, offer, channel, context of viewing, and success metric. Separate B2B and consumer audiences when their stakes, language, objections, or calls to action differ.

Read [references/narrative-patterns.md](references/narrative-patterns.md) to select an arc. Read [references/marketing-evidence.md](references/marketing-evidence.md) before making audience, competitor, market, performance, or trend claims.

## Build the message hierarchy

Write one central takeaway and no more than three supporting message pillars. Make each slide advance the same argument. Use concrete audience language, specific consequences, evidence, and a clear response path.

Avoid generic trend lists, adjective-heavy slogans, repeated agenda slides, fake urgency, invented personas, unsupported market leadership, and a catalog of features without meaning.

Use strong language only when it matches the brand and audience. Provocation must attack a costly behavior or broken method, then show the useful alternative; do not humiliate the customer.

## Compose and verify

Use the platform's native presentation capability. Follow an existing brand/template exactly when supplied. Use real product, customer, interface, and market evidence; do not generate fake proof or pseudo-official assets.

Render every slide and inspect it at full size. Verify that the opening creates relevance, the middle earns the conclusion, and the close asks for one realistic action. Record sources for claims and assets in notes when supported.
